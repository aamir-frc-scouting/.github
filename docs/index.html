2022
Rapid React Scout Lead · Learning

Where It Started

My freshman year, I didn't build the scouting app—I learned from people who did. Seniors Heath Crump and Caden Findlay led the development and brought me along, teaching me the ropes of competition scouting and why good data matters.

I served as a scout lead that season, running the tablet rotation in the stands and making sure we captured every match. It was my first taste of how scouting feeds directly into alliance selection strategy—and I fell in love with it.

That year, Team 1730 won the Newton Division at the Houston Championship and competed on Einstein as the 2nd pick of Alliance 3 (with teams 2910 and 973). We finished Rank 3 in the Round Robin. Seeing scouting data translate into a World Championship run showed me the real impact this work could have.

2023
Charged Up Solo Developer

Building Solo

Sophomore year, I took over as the team's sole scouting app developer. No more learning from others—I had to figure it out myself, in Java, building an Android app from scratch in Android Studio.

I struggled. A lot. The learning curve was steep: activities, intents, XML layouts, QR code libraries, getting the data format right so our strategists could actually use it. There were late nights before regionals fixing bugs I didn't understand. But I got it done.

The system worked: scouts filled out forms on Android tablets, each entry became a QR code, and we scanned those codes into an Excel workbook where each tab held a different data set. Offline-first and reliable under competition Wi-Fi rules.

2024
Crescendo Match + Pit Apps

Solving Another Bottleneck

Junior year, I noticed our pit scouting was chaos. Teams were using paper forms, losing data, duplicating work. If match scouting needed an app, pit scouting did too.

So I built a second app. Same stack—Java, Android—but designed for walking around the pits: capturing robot specs, capabilities, drivetrain details, and notes from conversations with other teams. The match app generated QR codes directly; the pit app wrote delimited !field1*field2*... strings to a text file, with QR codes generated externally for the scanner workflow.

Now I was maintaining two apps through the season, pushing updates between regionals, and making sure both data pipelines fed into our strategy meetings.

2025
Reefscape Flutter Rewrite

Going Cross-Platform

Senior year, I wanted to do it right. Three years of Java/Android had taught me a lot, but I wanted a more modern, professional setup. I rewrote both scouting apps from scratch in Dart/Flutter.

Flutter gave me cross-platform compatibility—the same codebase runs on Android, iOS, web, and desktop. The UI is cleaner, the code is more maintainable, and the development experience is faster. The mobile apps still generate QR codes with delimited *-separated fields—reliable under competition Wi-Fi restrictions.

But this year I also built the other side: a Python/customtkinter desktop intake app. A volunteer scans each QR string into a text box, the app validates all 23+ fields, and inserts rows into a SQLite MATCH_SCOUTING table. It also pulls Statbotics data for cross-referencing. We used it from Greater KC through Worlds—finally bridging offline QR collection with queryable SQL storage.

Why QR codes?

  • No Wi-Fi required — FRC competitions restrict network access
  • Instant transfer — no syncing, no waiting, no lost data
  • Reliable — works every time, even in crowded venues
  • Simple — any phone camera can read it
